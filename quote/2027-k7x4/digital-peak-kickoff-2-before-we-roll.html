01 The creative brief

Send us your answers before pre-production starts. Bullet points are fine. Honest answers help more than polished ones.

  1. What is this project for, and what should it accomplish?Bookings, awareness, recruitment, a launch, a sponsor deliverable…
  2. Who is the audience?Be specific. "Couples 30–50 planning a Keys getaway" is more useful than "travelers."
  3. What should someone feel, think or do after watching?
  4. Where will it live?Website, Instagram/TikTok, paid ads, broadcast, trade shows, in-room TV. This drives formats and music licensing.
  5. What are the deliverables?Lengths, aspect ratios (16:9, 9:16, 1:1), number of photos.
  6. What has to be in it?Must-have spaces, people, products, messaging, logos and legal lines.
  7. What should we avoid?Off-brand looks, competitor cues, areas under renovation, specific people or guests.
  8. What references do you love, and what do you hate?Links to videos, photos or brands. Tell us why for each one.
  9. Who approves the work, and who else needs to see it?
  10. What are the hard dates?Launch, campaign start, events, blackout dates, peak occupancy.

02 What to send us

  • Brand kit: logo files (vector or PNG), fonts, color codes and brand guidelines
  • Existing assets: past videos, photography and anything we should match or move away from
  • Contacts: on-site lead, chef/F&B lead, engineering or facilities, security, PR/social
  • Legal copy: required disclaimers, trademark lines and approval requirements
  • Music notes: genres, artists or tracks that fit the vibe. We'll license something in that lane

03 Locations & logistics

Access

  • Confirm access windows for every location on the shot list
  • Parking and a load-in path for carts and cases (in Key West this matters)
  • Power outlets near key setups, or let us know if we need to bring our own
  • A secure room for gear and a spot for the crew to eat
  • Give staff, security and neighbors a heads-up that we'll be filming

Hospitality & F&B

  • Rooms staged: fresh linens, no clutter, lamps working, blinds open
  • Plated dishes and cocktails ready at the scheduled times, with the chef or bartender available
  • Pools and common areas: guests notified, signage up, a plan for anyone who'd rather not be filmed
  • Out-of-order areas, construction or service carts moved out of frame
  • Any uniforms or branded items you want on camera

People on camera

  • Everyone recognizable on camera signs a release. We bring the forms
  • Talent and staff confirmed with call times
  • Guest privacy: we only feature people who've agreed to it
  • Wardrobe: solid colors, nothing neon, no small stripes or busy patterns
  • No visible competitor logos. Bring two or three outfit options
  • Hair and makeup ready by call time, or book our HMU artist
Drone shots: We're FAA Part 107 certified and handle all airspace authorization. Much of Key West sits in controlled airspace near Key West International and NAS Key West, so we need the location and dates at least 5 business days ahead. We'll also need the property's permission for takeoff and landing spots. Wind, rain and aircraft activity can ground a flight. Safety calls are final.

On the day

  • A standard day is 10 hours including lunch, measured from crew call to wrap.
  • Your on-set lead should be reachable all day and able to approve changes on the spot.
  • Want to watch? We'll set up a monitor so you can see the shots in real time.
  • We'll check in at lunch on progress against the shot list.
  • If weather or operations knock us off schedule, we'll reshuffle together and keep the must-haves first.
  • Footage is backed up twice before we leave the property.
