Commercial Video Production in the Florida Keys

September 3, 20245 min readBy Landon Jones

When it comes to commercial video production, few locations can captivate audiences quite like the Florida Keys. This tropical paradise, with its stunning scenery and unique culture, is a filmmaker's dream. From breathtaking sunsets over the Gulf of Mexico to the vibrant energy of Key West, this region offers an array of stunning backdrops for any project. However, while the Keys present idyllic visuals, they also pose several challenges that require careful planning and execution. In this blog post, we’ll explore the perks, the pretty, and the tough realities of commercial video production in the Florida Keys, along with insights on permits, tax breaks, and how to make the most of your experience.

The Pretty: Stunning Landscapes and Creative Vibes

One cannot talk about commercial video production in the Florida Keys without mentioning the captivating natural beauty that permeates every corner of the islands. Locations such as Key West, Islamorada, and Marathon each provide unique visual landscapes that can enhance any production.

Imagine filming against the backdrop of the Hemingway House, where nature and history collide, or capturing the iconic views from the Seven Mile Bridge, a stunning roadway that features panoramic views of turquoise waters. With its rustic charm and colorful architecture, Key West is not just a filming location; it’s a living canvas that invites creativity.

Beyond the urban scenes, the beautiful backcountry of the Florida Keys holds hidden treasures, accessible mainly by boat. These remote beaches and mangroves offer unique settings that are perfect for outdoor shoots. Picture yourself capturing stunning visuals as the sun sets behind palm trees, creating a serene ambiance that showcases the natural allure of the Keys.

The artistic community in the Florida Keys is vibrant and eclectic. Many talented creatives, including filmmakers, photographers, and visual artists, call this place home. The artistic atmosphere fosters collaboration—many local talents bring fresh perspectives that can enhance your production. Imagine the potential of partnering with local creatives who understand the cultural nuances of the islands, adding an authentic layer to your project.

The Perks: Year-Round Filming and Unique Themes

One of the significant advantages of commercial video production in the Florida Keys is the opportunity to shoot year-round. The tropical climate ensures warm temperatures and minimal rainfall, allowing filmmakers to maintain consistency in their visuals throughout different seasons. This stability is especially beneficial for ongoing campaigns or productions requiring multiple shoots, enabling you to recreate the same look and feel at various times of the year.

Additionally, the Keys are renowned for their aquatic themes. Whether it’s capturing thrilling water sports, sailing adventures, or showcasing the stunning marine life that calls these waters home, the filming possibilities are endless. Incorporating activities such as snorkeling, diving, or fishing into your storylines can elevate your project by highlighting the recreational opportunities available in this tropical paradise.

The Ugly: Travel Challenges and Logistics

While the Florida Keys are undeniably stunning, there are logistical challenges that filmmakers must navigate when planning a project. The drive from Miami to the Keys typically takes about 3.5 hours, but this journey can easily extend due to traffic, particularly during peak tourist seasons, making it essential to budget travel time carefully.

Limited flights to smaller airports—such as Key West International Airport—can complicate the logistics of bringing in talent and crew members. If you're working with a large production team, this could result in extended travel times and increased production costs.

Filming in the backcountry can also present logistical challenges. Securing boats for transportation and managing the crews needed for boating trips to remote locations adds another layer of complexity to production planning. Ensuring that all equipment is safely transported, handled, and stored can require extra attention, especially in the face of salty air and potential weather-related obstacles.

Navigating Permits and Tax Breaks

When considering commercial video production in the Florida Keys, filmmakers should be aware of local regulations, including obtaining the necessary permits. Depending on the scale of the production and the shooting locations, permits may be required from local authorities such as the City of Key West or Monroe County. These permits are crucial for ensuring that filming does not disrupt local life, and for complying with any environmental protections necessary for shooting in this unique landscape.

Engaging with a local production coordinator can simplify the permit application process, particularly if you are unfamiliar with the area. Having someone knowledgeable about local requirements can save you valuable time and enable you to focus on what you do best—creating amazing content.

In addition to the challenges of permits, it’s worthwhile to explore the tax breaks available for film productions in Florida. The state offers a Film and Entertainment Tax Incentive Program designed to attract filmmakers by providing tax credits for qualifying productions. Depending on the size and nature of your project, you may be able to reduce your overall production costs significantly. Consulting with a local film commission will help you understand how to capitalize on these incentives effectively.

Bringing in Talent: The Miami Connection

Casting talent is a crucial aspect of any commercial video production. Many filmmakers choose to bring in actors and crew from Miami due to its proximity and rich creative scene. This quick access can streamline your casting process, but travel logistics must be managed effectively to ensure all talent arrives on time.

Working with local actors can also add authenticity to your project. The Florida Keys have a unique vibe, and hiring locals can help convey that authenticity to your audience. Utilizing local talent brings fresh energy to your filming and connects your project more deeply to the region’s culture.

Most Filmed Spots in the Florida Keys

The Florida Keys boast numerous iconic filming locations that have been featured in both movies and television shows. Some of the most filmed spots include:

  • Key West: The vibrant streets and notable landmarks, such as Ernest Hemingway Home and Museum, have appeared in films like “True Lies” and “The Great Outdoors,” providing engaging backdrops for commercials and film projects alike.
  • John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park: Renowned for its stunning underwater scenery, this park is ideal for documentaries exploring marine life and conservation themes, giving filmmakers the chance to showcase Florida’s natural beauty.
  • The Dolphin Research Center: Located in Grassy Key, this center provides wonderful opportunities for heartwarming stories featuring dolphin interactions, ideal for commercials that connect with family values and nature.
  • The Overseas Highway: Famous for its breathtaking views, this highway serves as a picturesque backdrop in numerous productions, capturing the essence of the Florida Keys on film.

Conclusion

Commercial video production in the Florida Keys is a thrilling opportunity for filmmakers seeking to harness the incredible beauty and vibrant culture of this tropical paradise. While there are challenges related to travel logistics, permits, and weather conditions, the stunning landscapes, rich storytelling potential, and year-round filming opportunities make it a worthwhile pursuit.

By thoroughly planning your production, collaborating with local talent, and leveraging the unique aspects of the Keys, you can maximize your filming experience. Whether you’re creating commercials, music videos, or feature films, the Florida Keys offer a captivating setting that will enhance your narrative and resonate with your audience.
